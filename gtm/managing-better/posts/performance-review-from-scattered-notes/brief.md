# Brief: Reconstructing six months from half-memory

## Reader situation and search intent

A manager is approaching a review deadline and needs to prepare an employee's
performance review. The evidence exists, but it is split across CRM reports, 1:1
documents, development plans, the employee's self-review and the company's skills
matrix. A reusable spreadsheet has not survived the differences between roles or the
exceptions inside one person's results.

Primary search intent: practical guidance for how a manager prepares an employee
performance review when the supporting evidence is scattered.

Primary query: `how to prepare a performance review as a manager`

Nearby questions:

- What information should a manager gather for a performance review?
- How should a manager use an employee self-review?
- How can a manager account for context behind performance metrics?
- Can ChatGPT help write a performance review?

## Andrew's thesis

Writing the review is almost the last step. The harder job is assembling a fair,
role-specific account from systems that each hold one part of the period. A language
model can synthesize a prepared packet and produce a useful first draft. It cannot
know which missing exception would change the story.

Format: **Teardown**

Primary messaging pillar: **ready before the conversation**

Trigger moment: **review season**

## Raw material from Andrew

- "The hardest part for me ... is compiling all the information."
- CRM dashboards and reports are already set up to be filtered, but the result still
  needs interpretation.
- A customer transition or account handoff can change what a period of results means.
- Andrew often exports the data to Excel to work out the employee's actual results
  against expectations.
- A support role and a CSM role have different expectations, so a large,
  cross-functional team turns every review into a custom job.
- Prior attempts at a plug-and-play Google Sheet or Excel workbook have not removed
  the bespoke work.
- The qualitative record includes 1:1 documents, development plans where they exist,
  learning priorities, explicit requests for help and help from a teammate or mentor.
- The employee's self-review and the company's skills matrix supply two more lenses.
- ChatGPT has produced drafts that are roughly 70 percent complete once all the
  inputs are assembled. Andrew still edits and discovers missing details.
- A single review can take three hours.
- Afterward, Andrew resolves to build one system before the next cycle. Once the
  deadline passes, that work falls down the priority list.

Voice anchors from the original note, kept verbatim because the qualifications and
sentence movement matter:

- "To be fair, you know, I've created those so that they're easy to call up and
  filter. But there's always nuance when I look back at their period of results."
- "More often than not, come review time, it's a custom job. It's bespoke."
- "I'm looking at their development plan, which not every employee has, but some of
  them have."
- "GPT's usually gotten me, I don't know, maybe 70% of the way there. But there's
  always things I need to edit."
- "Every time the session ends, I think to myself, I should really lock in and commit
  to a single system so that next time when reviews come, it doesn't take me three
  hours to put one together for a single employee."

The post should not retain every filler word, but it should retain the qualification,
uncertainty, self-implication and longer clause structure underneath them.

## Source map

### Andrew's experience

All operational details, examples and the 70 percent / three-hour figures come from
Andrew's voice note supplied on 2026-09-01. They are first-person experience, not
general market claims.

### External sources and competing coverage

- [SHRM, How to Conduct a Great Performance Review](https://www.shrm.org/in/topics-tools/news/employee-relations/how-to-conduct-great-performance-review)
  covers standards, preparation and paying attention to behavior before the review.
- [Lattice, Mastering the Performance Review Conversation](https://lattice.com/articles/mastering-the-performance-review-conversation)
  advises managers to eliminate surprises, provide examples and find themes.
- [Lattice, Preparing for Performance Reviews as a First-Time Manager](https://people.lattice.com/rs/372-AAD-485/images/Workbook%20-%20Preparing%20for%20Performance%20Reviews%20as%20a%20First-Time%20Manager.pdf)
  is directly adjacent to the first-time-manager intent.

The competing coverage concentrates on what to collect and how to conduct the
conversation. This piece contributes the messier step before synthesis: role-specific
data reconciliation, exceptions and the joins between several incomplete records.

### The Same Page's assertion

The missing system is a continuing record, not a better end-of-cycle writing
template. That is an editorial argument consistent with the product point of view;
the post presents it as Andrew's conclusion rather than a researched fact.
