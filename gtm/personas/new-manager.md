# Persona — the new manager

The one persona that matters for v1. Everything here traces to a verbatim quote in
`gtm/research/audience-2026-08.md`; nothing is invented. Read before writing copy for
any surface, and before a product decision about who this is for.

**The current site copy is scaffolding, not a fixed point.** It was written to get a
site standing pre-launch, it is unpublished, and no line in it constrains this work.

### Sharpened ICP

| Attribute | Where it lands |
|---|---|
| Reports | 4–11. Below ~4, memory still works and nobody buys. |
| Tenure | Under 3 years managing, **and has survived at least one review cycle**. |
| Context | Tech company, remote/hybrid, no internal manager coaching. |
| Current system | Already has a doc per person. Already behind on it. Has already abandoned at least one alternative. |
| Not our buyer | HR. A manager with 1–2 reports. A manager whose system works. |

### The homemade system they already run

1. One container per person (doc, OneNote page, Notion page, spreadsheet tab).
2. Reverse-chronological dated entries, newest at top.
3. A running open-items list above the log.
4. A second, private container for comp, HR risk and personal details.
5. An annual scramble to convert it into a review (1–2 hours per person).

What it lacks is recurrence, carry-forward and prompting. Those are supplied by
the manager's memory, which is the part that breaks.

### Three distinct failure modes (they need different product answers)

- **The habit decays.** "It's something i've always wanted to do, but never had the time to"
- **Retrieval fails.** "The hard part is of course finding the links to things."
- **The annual conversion is brutal.** "For each person I'm basically trying to reconstruct six months from half-memory."

### The creepy dossier problem — the emotional core

Two managers, independently, in different threads, called their own private notes
a "creepy dossier." The manager wants the record and feels like a bad person for
keeping it. **That ambivalence is why the habit decays**, not only busyness.

The resolution is stated by a manager, unprompted: *"Its not 'surveillance' if
you tell them they own the doc as well."*

Sharing the record is not a privacy concession. It is the mechanism that makes
the manager willing to keep the record at all. It is the product, and it is
already the name.

The same post asks the product's question in one sentence: *"How do those of you
with more than a handful of reports actually do this? without it turning into
surveillance?"* — verified verbatim against source.

### The incumbent's specific failure

The only place managers criticise Lattice at length is HR visibility into 1:1
notes. The documented outcome is that managers **write less honestly**: "Keep
your notes as objective as possible - leave out commentary." An HR-owned tool
and a manager-owned tool shared with one report are categorically different
objects.

### What they believe

- **"The tool doesn't matter, discipline does."** The belief that stops the sale.
  Copy cannot move it; the product has to survive an undisciplined week.
- The books stop working exactly at the hard part (the person who isn't working out).
- What actually helps is a more experienced manager to ask.
- Being called a micromanager is the cardinal insult; some stop checking in entirely to avoid it.
- They can't calibrate: "It's really hard for me to even assess what's reasonable or not."
  The gap is well evidenced. What to *call* it is a separate question the evidence
  does not answer.
- Standards erode by avoidance and they know it: "I would let way too much slide and
  lowered the bar more than I should have." Note the direction: he had a bar and
  watched it slide. That is a different story from "define the bar."

### The AI line (standing rule)

**AI is welcome upstream of the manager's judgment and offensive at or after it.
The boundary is saving, not drafting.**

- Fine: recall, retrieval, prep, and a draft scored against the manager's own scale
  from evidence the manager can see.
- Never: saving a value the manager didn't set; scoring from activity metrics;
  filling a gap the evidence doesn't support.
- Worse than not doing it: doing any of it undisclosed.

The earlier wording banned "producing a rating" while permitting "a draft the manager
rewrites," which left a drafted rating in both columns. See `gtm/brand/voice-rules.md`
for the settled line.

Differentiator against "just use Claude": an LLM tells you what happened. It has
no standard to measure against, no continuity between sessions, and nothing the
report can see.

### Trigger moments (in order)

1. Review season. Calendar-driven, so acquisition has a shape.
2. Crossing roughly five reports. "It worked until it didn't."
3. Inheriting a team with zero notes from the previous manager.
4. The first person who isn't working out.
5. A conversation they're still replaying.

## The original ICP, preserved

The pre-research definition, from the Miro board. The sharpened version above refines
it rather than replacing it, and the two differ in ways worth keeping visible.

> First-time or newly-promoted manager with 3+ direct reports, at a tech-industry
> company (startup or scale-up), on a GTM team, with under 3 years of management
> experience, working remote, at a company without a strong internal manager-coaching
> culture. Age: roughly 28–35. Pain: balancing multiple demands in the role while
> still figuring out how to fairly assess team performance against ambiguous goals.
>
> **What they are NOT:** an HR team, a VP of People, or a seasoned manager who's done
> this for 10 years. Design and message for the new manager figuring it out, not the
> expert who already has a system.

**What the research changed:** 3+ reports became 4–11, because below roughly four the
manager's memory still works and nobody buys anything. "Under 3 years managing" gained
a second condition, having survived at least one review cycle, because that is the
trigger. Everything else held.

**What the research could not test:** the age band and the GTM-team attribute. The
corpus is engineering-heavy and only three first-person GTM-manager accounts were
found. Both attributes stand on the board's original judgment, not on evidence.

## The objection that kills the sale

"IME The tool really doesn't matter; but proper discipline to use it does." Upvoted,
widely held. **Copy cannot move this.** It is a product question: what happens in week
three when they haven't opened the app? Without an answer, the positioning is writing
a cheque the product has to cash.
