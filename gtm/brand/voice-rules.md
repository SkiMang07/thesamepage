# Voice rules

The binding language rules, as far as they have been established. **This is not the
finished voice system.** Layers 3 (point of view) and 4 (message hierarchy) come
first; the full voice system is layer 5 and is not written yet. What is here binds now
and will be absorbed into that document rather than replaced by it.

Every rule below traces to evidence in `gtm/research/audience-2026-08.md` or to a
decision Andrew made and recorded.

---

## The register

Given ~25 headline options across six angles, Andrew picked zero from the "lonely in
the middle" group and zero that framed the reader as deficient. Every pick clustered
on clarity, evidence, and what good looks like.

**How to apply:** the reader is a manager who wants to be good at this and expects to
be addressed as a professional. No hand-holding, no "we know it's hard," no
empathy-first framing. Craft and rigor, not comfort. Also rejected: cold, accusatory
phrasing. Confident and even, aimed at neither pity nor blame.

## The grammar rule

**Say what they said, in the grammar they said it in. First person and observational,
never second person and imperative.**

Managers write about themselves in the first person and observationally, and they
blame themselves heavily and unprompted ("I dont think I can just chalk it up to being
disorganized"). A reader already indicting himself does not need a second-person
imperative doing it for him.

Worked example: "Stop reconstructing the year from memory" has the right content and
the wrong grammar. "Reconstructing six months from half-memory" is a real manager's
sentence about himself.

## The surveillance verb

Use **see**. Never *watch*, *track*, or *monitor*. The product is one bad verb away
from reading as employee surveillance, which the research establishes as a documented
category risk rather than a hunch. Applies everywhere: marketing copy, product UI,
blog, field labels.

## The AI line

AI is welcome **upstream** of the manager's judgment and offensive at or after it.
Recall, retrieval, prep, a draft the manager rewrites: fine. Producing or shading a
rating: not. Undisclosed: worse than not doing it. Never write copy implying the
product scores or judges a person.

## The original messaging principles, preserved

From the board, and all three still hold.

- Lead with the manager's pain, not the product's features.
- "Mission control for your team" is the right metaphor — single surface, everything
  you need to be a confident manager. *(See the unverified table below: this is our
  metaphor, never tested against the corpus.)*
- Avoid HR-speak ("performance management," "engagement scores"). Use manager-speak
  ("how's my team doing," "what do I say in this conversation"). *(The research turned
  this principle into the specific say/never-say lists below.)*

## Do not lead on time saved

The weakest claim available, and the one axis where a free LLM already wins. Lead on
the work being right and unsurprising; time saved is a benefit, not the argument.

## Say / never say

| Say | Never say |
|---|---|
| my team, my reports, one of my reports | your workforce, your people, talent, headcount |
| 1:1 (digits) | check-in, touchpoint, meeting cadence |
| hard conversation, tough conversation | difficult conversation, crucial conversation |
| doing a good job, where they stand | performance management, engagement score, eNPS |
| notes, context, prep | track, monitor, watch, visibility into, dashboard |
| what's reasonable, how fast someone should work | competency framework, leveling, career architecture |
| they said they'd do it and they didn't | commitment tracking, accountability culture, action items |
| I forget, I can't remember, half-memory | recency bias, single source of truth, actionable insights |

"Recency bias" is known-but-clinical: a tech manager will recognise it and would
never reach for it to describe their own week.

### Ours, and unverified

Do not present these as the reader's language. They may still be the right choice;
they have to win on merit rather than on authenticity.

| Phrase | What the corpus shows |
|---|---|
| the bar | **One** instance in ~120 quotes, in the opposite direction: "lowered the bar more than I should have." One instance is a lead, not vocabulary. |
| what good looks like | **Zero** instances anywhere in the corpus. |
| mission control | The old GTM.md called "mission control for your team" the right metaphor. Never tested against the corpus and not carried forward automatically. Same status as the bar: ours, and it has to win on merit. |
| what I owe them | Zero instances as a phrase. It came from a research pass's own synthesis, not a quote. |

## Andrew's own prose rules

Apply to any long-form copy written in his voice.

- No em-dashes. Use spaced hyphens or ellipses, sparingly.
- Never begin a sentence or clause with "not", and never begin a sentence with "But".
- Use contractions.
- Headlines stay clipped. Body prose is warmer, longer and looser than the headlines.
