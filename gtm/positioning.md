## The competitive alternative

**Lattice, 15Five and Culture Amp are not the competitive alternative.** Across
five independent threads spanning 2020–2026, zero managers named a
performance-management product as the tool they actually use to run 1:1s and
remember their people. Those products are what the *company* bought and what the
manager copy-pastes into at review time.

**The real alternative is a doc per person plus the manager's memory** — Google
Docs, OneNote, Notion, Excel, Trello, a paper notebook. Increasingly it is a doc
per person plus an LLM pointed at Jira/Slack/Docs, which is fast at recall and
has nothing to measure against. That LLM path is the fastest-growing competitor
and `gtm/business-model.md` does not mention it.

### The differentiation thesis, preserved

Also from the board. Point 1 survives the research intact and is now the strongest
claim we have. Points 2 and 3 have never been tested against the audience.

1. **Manager-first** — every competitor is sold to HR/People teams. We design for the
   manager as the primary user and buyer. *(Confirmed: the Lattice privacy thread
   shows HR visibility making managers write less honestly.)*
2. **All-in-one for the manager** — competitors are single-purpose; managers stitch
   4-5 tools together. We collapse 1:1 notes, commitments, performance context, and
   coaching nudges into one surface. *(Untested. The evidence shows managers running
   one doc per person rather than 4-5 tools, so this claim may be solving a problem
   they do not have.)*
3. **Judgment, not just data** — we don't just show data, we help the manager know
   what to do with it. AI-assisted coaching and prep, not dashboards. *(Untested against the audience, and it collides with the AI line:
   judgment is exactly where managers draw a hard boundary.)*

## Current positioning

- **Competitive alternative** *(finding)* - a doc per person plus the manager's
  memory, increasingly with a general-purpose LLM used for recall.
- **Unique attributes** *(assertion)* - management context across work,
  expectations and conversations; continuity between moments; traceable reasons for
  where to look more closely; AI assistance that stops before the manager's decision.
- **Value** *(assertion)* - a manager can put the pieces together, ask a better
  question and decide how to respond with a sound basis.
- **Who cares a lot** *(finding)* - the manager whose own system has stopped keeping
  up, typically after the team grows beyond what memory can reliably hold.
- **Market category** *(assertion, still open)* - a management context system for the
  individual manager. This needs plain language before it becomes public copy.
### The current statement

> For a manager whose home-made system has stopped keeping up, The Same Page keeps
> the context across their team's work, expectations and conversations coherent, so
> they can see where to look more closely and decide how to respond.

This statement is deliberately broader than any one workflow. Preparation,
follow-ups and reviews demonstrate the value without defining the category.

## Earlier positioning frame, preserved

Read this layer differently from the one above it. Layer 1 is evidence and can
contradict us. **Layer 2 cannot be free, because the product is already built.**
Competitive alternative and who-cares-a-lot are findings. The other three are
assertions chosen by me; the test on each is whether it is the sharpest true thing we
could say, not whether the research produced it.

- **Competitive alternative** *(finding)* — a doc per person, plus your memory. Increasingly plus an LLM.
- **Unique attributes** *(assertion)* — shared by construction · a standard set in advance · continuity you don't maintain · AI that stops before judgment.
- **Value** *(assertion)* — you keep the record without feeling like you're compiling a file on your own team, and the year is already written down when you sit down to write it.
- **Who cares a lot** *(finding)* — the manager whose own system has already broken.
- **Market category** *(assertion, weakest)* — the manager's own record of their team, shared with the team. Rejected: "performance management" (HR's category, wrong buyer) and "1:1 tool" (too small, and a graveyard: Charma, Kona, Hypercontext, Know Your Team).

### The statement

> For a manager whose doc-per-person has stopped keeping up, The Same Page is the
> record you keep **with** each person rather than about them, against a standard
> you set in advance, so the review is already written and nobody is surprised by it.

Passes Harry Dry's three: visualisable, falsifiable, and no competitor could sign
it, because every one of them sells to the person the manager is hiding the doc from.

This is deliberately narrower than the product. Expectations, capacity, projects
and Scribe are not in that sentence. Positioning is what you lead with.

## What the earlier research settles, and what it does not

### The shared-record mechanism is evidenced. Its causal importance is not validated.

**Supported:** managers described private notes as a "creepy dossier," and one
manager named co-ownership as a way to resolve the surveillance feeling. A second
described a shared document working, and a third named "no surprises at review time"
as the goal.

**Not established:** that dossier guilt is the primary cause of habit decay, that a
shared record resolves it reliably, or that this mechanism should lead the homepage.
The corpus is small and engineering-heavy, and the employee-facing product is not
built.

**Does not follow:** that "You and the person you manage should be looking at the
same page" is therefore the right hero line. The evidence backs a mechanism. It says
nothing about which sentence carries it or whether the hero is where it belongs.
**The H1 is open.** An earlier draft of this doc declared it closed; that was a
synthesis error, not a finding.

### Earlier surface lead, superseded 2026-09-26

Posed as a choice between review season and the dossier guilt. It was never one slot.
They do different jobs, and the channel forces the split rather than taste deciding it.

| Job | Which one, and why |
|---|---|
| **Acquisition** (blog, SEO, why they arrive) | **Review season.** Forced, not preferred: *the guilt has no search query.* Nobody googles "I feel weird keeping notes on my team." They google things about a review they're behind on. Content is the entire GTM for v1, so top of funnel sits where demand exists. |
| **Positioning** (homepage, why they choose us) | **Superseded:** the homepage now leads with coherent context for better management decisions. See `gtm/site/homepage.md`. |
| **Retention** (why they renew) | Continuity and accumulated context, with preparation and reviews as visible payoffs rather than the whole value. |

**The bridge, so the site does not read as two products.** The founding post already
contains the causal chain, in this order: the review felt off *because* he is
reconstructing from half-memory, *because* his note-keeping is wildly inconsistent,
*because* keeping it feels like building a creepy dossier. Stated as one line:

> The reason the review is agony in November is that the record you needed in March
> felt creepy to keep.

**Do not lead the review story on time saved.** It is the weakest claim we have and
the one axis where we lose to something free — "1-2h per employee to gather all the
data, now gone" is a manager describing what an LLM already did for him. What an LLM
cannot do is hold a standard set in advance or produce a record the report has already
seen. Lead on the review being *right* and unsurprising; time saved comes second.

### The register conflict, which the evidence does settle

Managers write about themselves in the first person and observationally, and they
blame themselves heavily and unprompted ("I dont think I can just chalk it up to
being disorganized"). A reader already indicting himself does not need a
second-person imperative doing it for him.

**Standing rule: say what they said, in the grammar they said it in.** First person
and observational, never second person and imperative.

Worked example: "Stop reconstructing the year from memory" has the right content and
the wrong grammar. "Reconstructing six months from half-memory" is a real manager's
sentence about himself; as an imperative aimed at the reader it becomes an accusation.
"They made you a manager. Nobody made you ready." is cut for the same reason.
