A bit about me

I've been managing people for over a decade now. Teams of three and teams of 23, managers of other managers, people in their very first job and subject matter experts who'd been in their field for more than 20 years. I've been obsessed with getting better at it the whole way along, and I've tried more systems than I'd like to admit to keep myself organized and make the conversations with the person on the other side of the table (or the Zoom call) genuinely useful.

I'm a big believer in a good, consistent system, and I lean on data and analysis for most of my decisions, especially when nothing's on fire. So I guess it's not all that surprising that I ended up blending the ops and systems side of me with the part of managing I love most, which is helping people learn, get better at their day-to-day and grow in their careers.

I've never really stopped thinking of myself as a student. I'm usually reading two or three books at a time (okay, two are audiobooks, but... come on), and between podcasts, YouTube, conferences and shadowing people who are better at something than I am, I ask a lot of questions. I think being humble enough to know you don't know everything, and soaking up what other people do know, is one of the most important habits you can build. It's also something I try hard to pass on to my teams, so learning and development usually sits front and center in my 1:1s, along with a plan for how that learning turns into the next step in someone's career.

Most of my career has been in Customer Success, and I love it. Figuring out how to set a team up so customers really get value from what they're paying for is a puzzle I still enjoy. I've worked on everything from a rapidly changing SaaS product to helping people who spend most of their day a long way from a computer see why a piece of technology is worth their time.

The Same Page is kind of the culmination of all that. It pulls a handful of tools I'd been cobbling together for myself over the years into one place. My hope is that one day it's more than a piece of software, and becomes a way of managing that helps a lot of people be better at this than I was when I started.
