Four ways out of the wall of text.

Same argument, same eight beats. The question is what the page does while it makes the argument. Grey boxes are where copy goes. Drag, scroll and pull the things that look draggable.

Why this happened: the research said run the argument before showing the product. I turned that into "text before everything," which is a different thing and a worse one. None of these four abandon the argument. They stop setting it like a document.

01

The bar is a real object, and it runs the whole page.

Your brand is already a physical metaphor and the page currently ignores it. Make the bar a line that persists from the header to the footer: it moves as you scroll, evidence marks rise to meet it, and in one section the visitor grabs it and sets it themselves. The H1, the name and the product mechanic become one visual idea.

Build cost medium · one SVG plus scroll math · nothing to maintain once set

Set the bar where you think it belongs. 6 of 8 clear it. Move it and the answer changes, which is the entire argument for writing it down once.

02

The scroll builds the record in front of you.

The page claims evidence accumulates on its own. So let the reader watch it happen. A rail pins alongside the argument and fills as they scroll, one entry at a time. By the bottom they have watched a year assemble without anyone sitting down to write a report. The proof is the interaction, not a sentence about the interaction.

03

Show the mess. Let them wipe it away.

Beat two describes a spreadsheet, an abandoned Notion page and a Notes file. Right now that is a paragraph. Draw it instead: scattered scraps at angles on the left, the same week as one clean record on the right, and a handle the visitor drags between them. It is the before and after of the whole product in one gesture, and it needs no screenshot of anything you have to keep true.

04

No gimmick. Make the typography the architecture.

The honest control in this set. Zero interaction, zero scroll tricks, nothing to maintain. The page stops looking like a document because the type stops behaving like one: display sizes that break the grid, an asymmetric column structure, marginalia, and pull quotes set at a size that makes them events rather than decoration. This is the 37signals route, and it is the only one of the four that costs nothing to keep alive.

What I'd actually do

These are not four pages. Three of them stack. 04 is the floor — do the typographic work regardless, because it fixes "bland" on its own and costs nothing to keep. Then add 03 at beat two, where you already have a paragraph describing exactly that mess, and 01 as the spine that ties the name, the headline and the product mechanic together.

02 is the one I'd hold. It is the most impressive and the most fragile, it fights the reader for attention while they are trying to read the best writing on the page, and it is the one that will still be half-broken on someone's phone in six months.
