## 1. What the name means

The single most important thing on the board, and it was nowhere in the repo:

> We provide all the tools that good managers use to get on the same page with
> their teams and build high performing teams.
>
> Culture of trust, accountability, transparency — helps people understand what
> good looks like, where they stack up, how they can improve. Helps focus your
> time on the "right" segments of the team and manage out those that are not
> living up to role expectations.
>
> **Everyone needs to be on the same page: the employee and manager, the manager
> and director, and the director and C-suite.**

That last line is the product name's reason for existing. It is a homepage
headline or the line directly under one.

## 3. Proof — third-party, sourced

There is no customer proof yet, but there is a great deal of research proof, and
it is specific and attributable. This is the evidence layer for the problem
section.

**Managers are the variable that matters**
- Managers account for **at least 70% of the variance** in employee engagement (Gallup)
- **50% of employees leave their company because of their boss** — 1 in 2 US adults
  have left a job to get away from a manager (Gallup, n=7,272)
- 50% of employees experience culture most strongly through their manager's
  approach to performance

**Managers are not equipped**
- Nearly **two-thirds of managers are highly confident** in their ability to lead —
  **only one-third of employees** share that confidence
- **69% of managers are uncomfortable communicating with employees**; 37% are
  uncomfortable giving direct performance feedback
- **68% of managers are overwhelmed** (Gartner, 75 HR leaders) — and only **14%**
  of those companies had taken any step to help
- **83% of founders are first-time founders**, so the management culture they set
  is itself inexperienced

**The expectation gap is measurable**
- 57% of managers say their org offers clear advancement tracks — **only 36% of
  employees agree**; 47% say it does not exist or they are unsure
- 85% of managers say they have discussed career vision with a report —
  **only 52% of employees** say the same
- Only **30% of US employees are engaged** (13% worldwide), barely moved in 12 years

**Performance management is broken and everyone knows it**
- **Only 2% of companies** feel their performance management approach delivers
  exceptional value

**And the mechanism works when it happens**
- **Over 71% of employees who have weekly performance conversations are highly
  engaged**

Sources on the board: Gallup (State of the American Workplace; "Why Great Managers
Are Rare"), HBR ("What Great Managers Do", "The Performance Management
Revolution", "Managers Can't Do It All"), Gartner via Forbes, Bain, Deloitte,
Investopedia. Full link list in the Research section of the CSV.

**Verify every number before publishing.** These were collected around 2024 and
several cite studies older than that. Anything that goes on the site needs a live
source check and a date.

## 4. The positioning frame

The board's four questions, with what was written under them:

**What's unworkable**
- Missing "root cause" data on employees — seeing what they are doing against what
  they should be doing

**What's unavoidable**
- Lack of engaged managers leads to disengaged employees
- Business growth gets stunted

**What's urgent**
- Managers, especially frontline, are the key intersection for bringing scale-ups
  through the journey
- Managers are the translators of company vision into execution — bringing the
  vision to the frontline

**How it's underserved in the market**
- Missing a *target manager* strategy to address this root problem

That is a complete problem argument: it doesn't work → it compounds → it's urgent
now → nobody is solving it for this buyer.

## 5. Two audiences, two value props

**For managers** — the headline words on the board are **Clarity**,
**Transparency**, **Get organized**:
- Provide a proven approach that leaders of high performing teams use, and the
  tools to back it up
- Provide an objective and fair approach to performance evaluations
- Pull the three most important elements of performance management — metrics,
  skills, behaviors — into one place
- Clarify what "good" looks like, measure where employees are against it, drive
  accountability on where they need to focus
- Stay focused and organized against the performance that really matters
- Infrastructure for engagement and trust · a management foundation for success

**For business / department heads**
- Empower leaders with 360-degree team performance insight, enabling informed
  decision-making and strategic team development
- A culture of trust, accountability and transparency — helps people understand
  what good looks like, where they stack up, and how to improve; helps focus time
  on the right segments of the team

## 6. Frameworks the board used

**BLAC & white** — is the pain Blatant, Latent, Critical, Aspirational; a vitamin
or a penicillin; before/after; acute pain vs absolute joy. Positioning template as
written:

> For **(minimum viable segment)** — with BLAC, that is 4U
> Who are dissatisfied with **(the current unworkable or underserved alternative)**
> Our product is a **(new product category)**
> That provides **(compelling switching capability / gain)** that overcomes **(the risk)**
> Unlike **(the unworkable alternative that underserves)**

Filling this in is probably the fastest route to a headline.

**Evaluation** — opportunity must be Disruptive, Discontinuous (different enough),
Defensible. Adoption framed as pain-of-adoption/cost vs gain-of-purchase.

**Product positioning strategy — three key benefits**, as written:
1. **Centralized performance indicators** — all key indicators in one platform
2. **Enhanced transparency for superiors** — customizable views to gauge team
   performance across the org
3. **Streamlined management infrastructure** — a structured template for effective
   team management, building confidence and competency

## 7. What's unique (board's own list)

All-in-one with seamless integrations · recommended performance rating · AI
generated performance review · **designed with the manager in mind, not the HR
team** · usability is paramount · transparent "cards" to indicate performance and
skills.

## 8. Beyond the app — offers and content

The board framed this as "not just an app":
- **"The StartUp Manager"** — community, blog, videos
- Online courses, workshops, user groups; "doesn't have to be lonely in the middle"
- Training and coaching for first-time managers, and for managing remote teams
- Consulting on best practices for engaging your team

Directly relevant to the offers and support pages.

A **data play** was also sketched — anonymized benchmarking on manager and team
performance, and on capacity by role. Long-horizon; not site material yet, but it
explains the "monetize data + benchmarks" line in the vision.

## 10. Open questions this does not answer

- **What the CTA is.** Free trial, waitlist, or demo. Still undecided, still the
  thing that shapes the page most.
- **Which audience the homepage speaks to first.** The board has two value props;
  GTM says the buyer is the individual manager, not the department head. The
  homepage should probably commit to the manager and let the department-head
  argument live on a secondary page.
- **Whether to lead with the name's meaning** ("everyone on the same page") or with
  the sharper operational pain ("you can't tell how your people are actually doing").
