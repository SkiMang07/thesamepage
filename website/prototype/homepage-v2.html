02  The week02 / 08

Tell me if this is your week.

You have six people. Six recurring 1:1s that you move at least twice a month. Your actual record of them is a spreadsheet HR sent you in January, a Notion page you started in March and gave up on by April, and a Notes file you wouldn't want anyone reading over your shoulder.

Someone asks how Priya is doing and you say she's doing great. You mean it. You just can't say why in a way that would survive a follow-up question.

Then October comes and you have to write her review. You scroll back through Slack looking for June. What you end up writing is honest and thin, and you know it's thin.

You keep meaning to take better notes. There's nowhere for them to go.

03  The argument03 / 08

Two things have to be true first.

If you disagree with either one, the rest of this probably isn't worth paying for.

01

What good looks like has to live outside your head

A paragraph per role, in the words you'd use to their face. Most of us carry it around unwritten and quietly assume everyone else is working from the same one. They're not.

02

The record has to build itself

If keeping the record costs you a Sunday, you won't keep it. I know because I didn't, for about three years.

04  The plan04 / 08

Define the bar.
Then see who clears it.

Three moves.

Move01

Write the bar

Once, per role and per level. Paste in the job description you already have and edit it into words you'd actually use. Then send it to them. A bar they can't see is a tripwire.

Move02

Work normally

1:1s, check-ins and commitments land against the person as you go. Your written expectations ground everything it drafts for you. Nothing gets written about a person until you've read it.

Move03

Open Monday to one thing

One suggestion, with the reason sitting next to it. The ranking is rules you can read, not a model's opinion, and everything behind it is one click away.
