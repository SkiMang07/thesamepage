# Search and AI-answer review

SEO and visibility in AI answers share one foundation: original, accurate, crawlable
content that resolves a real question. Do not add a separate layer of prose for a
machine or claim a special AI schema exists.

## Before drafting

- Identify the primary query and the job behind it.
- Inspect current results, not a remembered keyword list.
- Note which questions the existing results answer well and where they become generic.
- Check the Managing Better backlog and completed packages for overlap.
- Decide what Andrew can contribute that a commodity summary cannot: a real example,
  teardown, script, distinction, concession or decision rule.
- Collect primary sources for material factual claims. Record publication and event
  dates where freshness matters.

## In the post

- Make the title and opening recognizably about the question the reader searched.
- State the central answer early without using a canned definition paragraph.
- Give important passages enough context to stand alone when quoted.
- Prefer named objects, dates, behavior and direct examples over broad adjectives.
- Use descriptive headings. Questions are headings only when a reader would naturally
  ask them.
- Use lists and tables when the relationships are genuinely clearer in that form.
- Link to relevant Managing Better pieces with descriptive anchor text.
- Cite the source beside the claim it supports. Never add a source list that leaves
  the reader guessing which statement came from which source.

## Metadata

- The HTML title can differ from the visible title when a clearer search phrase helps,
  but both must promise the same article.
- Keep the slug short, stable and specific. Omit dates unless time is intrinsic.
- The post summary earns the click on the blog index. It is not a duplicate of the
  opening paragraph.
- The meta description accurately states what the reader will get and uses the primary
  language naturally. Treat length indicators as display guidance, not a ranking hack.
- Alt text describes the final meaningful image. It does not repeat the keyword.

## Final checks

- The piece fully satisfies one intent rather than partially satisfying several.
- The answer contains something specific enough to cite or remember.
- Facts, inference, Andrew's experience and company point of view are distinguishable.
- No unsupported superlative, prevalence claim, result or product promise remains.
- No FAQ exists solely to repeat keywords or chase schema.
- Any suggested structured-data change belongs to the shared template, not the body.
